Methodology

How we calculate the true cost

One simple model, applied the same way in every city, so the numbers are comparable.

The promo trap, in plain terms

  • The offer: a low first-month or web-only rate on select units, for new customers only.
  • The fees: a one-time admin fee (commonly $25–$50), a required protection plan or insurance ($10–$30 a month), a lock, sometimes a deposit.
  • The increase: most rentals are month-to-month. Large operators send existing-customer rate increases with 30 days’ notice; increases of 30–40% are widely reported, sometimes within the first four months.
  • Why it works: moving a full unit takes a truck, a day and help. Operators know most people stay.

The six-month model

For each unit size we take the average non-climate street rate from two or three public price pages, then model six months:

  1. Month 1: a promo at 50% of the market rate. A “$1” promo is cheaper, but often only on select units.
  2. Months 2–4: the market rate.
  3. Months 5–6: the market rate plus a 30% existing-customer increase. We also show the range for +15% to +40%.
  4. Plus: a ~$29 one-time admin fee and a ~$12 monthly protection plan.

6-month true average = (all rent + fees + protection) ÷ 6. We also show rent only. Every city page has a calculator so you can test a real quote.

Sources and estimates

We use public pages from StorageCafe (Yardi Matrix data), StorageUnitCosts, Storage Stats, SpareFoot and operator blogs such as Extra Space. Where a size has only one source, or no public lowest-promo figure, the table says so and the figure should be treated as an estimate. Metro-wide averages can be lower than city averages because they include suburbs.

ZIP codes and neighborhoods

The ZIP check uses the Census Bureau’s 2020 ZIP Code Tabulation Areas, which approximate USPS ZIP codes. When about half or more of that area’s land sits inside a city we publish, the result opens that city page. The rates do not change. We do not publish a dollar figure for a ZIP code. Partial overlap is labeled with the land share. Cities farther than 60 straight-line miles are left off, because borrowing their average would pretend to a precision we do not have.

Some city pages also list area street rates StorageCafe published, with the date and the unit size that sentence names. Those are lower-priced and higher-priced areas they named, not a central / suburban / outer ring, and not a match to your ZIP. Where StorageCafe did not publish that list, the page says neighborhood rates are not on file.

What the model does not know

Some facilities lock rates for 6–12 months; some never raise them early. Climate control, upper floors, neighborhood and season all move the price. The point is to compare like with like — the month-four price, not the month-one price.
