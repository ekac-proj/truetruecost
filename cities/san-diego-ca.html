What a storage unit in San Diego really costs

San Diego listings advertise non-climate 10x10 units from about $39, while typical street rates run about $174 in the city and about $220 across the metro. Coastal suburbs pull the metro figure up. Climate-controlled 10x10 street rates sit close to standard units here, but fees and the first existing-customer increase still put the six-month average above the banner price.

How to avoid the promo trap

  1. Ask how long the rate is locked. "When is the earliest my rent can go up, and by how much was the last increase here?" Get it in writing.
  2. Ask for the full move-in total. Admin fee, required protection, lock, deposit, tax. A "$1 first month" can still cost $40–$80 to move in.
  3. Compare the month-4 price, not month 1. Run each quote through the calculator.
  4. Get two or three competing quotes for the same size and climate control, including at least one independent facility.
  5. Use your own insurance if allowed. Many renters and homeowners policies cover off-site storage; some facilities waive their plan with proof.
  6. Negotiate when the increase notice arrives. You usually get 30 days. Quote the current new-customer rate for your size and ask them to match it.
  7. Be ready to move or downsize. If the gap to the new-customer rate is large, a promo elsewhere can pay for a truck within two or three months.
  8. Prepay only if the rate is locked. Prepay discounts mean little if rent can still rise mid-term.
  9. Set a reminder for month three. That is when increase notices commonly start to arrive.

Notes for San Diego

  • StorageCafe lists Lincoln Park (~$153), Fairmont Park, Palm City and Torrey Pines Preserve (~$162) among the cheapest areas, while Bernardo Springs (~$349) and Carmel Mountain (~$316) run highest.
  • Nearby cities on the same September 2026 street-rate cut: Encinitas about $201 and Chula Vista about $190 for a non-climate 10x10, against $174 in the city of San Diego. That spread is why the metro average ($220) sits above the city figure.
  • San Diego's all-unit average is lowest around January (~$196) and highest in August (~$204), StorageCafe.
  • The city has about 4.1 sq ft of storage per person and 80 facilities (StorageCafe). Only about 91,000 sq ft opened in 2025, so promos are thinner than in markets still adding supply.
  • On StorageCafe's figures, climate-controlled units in San Diego can price at or below standard units. Price both before paying extra for climate.
  • Extra Space charges a $50 admin fee (per StorageUnitCosts), higher than our $29 default; adjust the calculator if your facility charges more.
