Size guide

What size storage unit do you need?

A 5x5 is a closet. A 10x20 is a one-car garage. These are the sizes our city pages price. The quiz maps your situation onto one of them, then you can compare the six-month true cost — not the first-month promo.

What do you need?

Three questions: what you are storing, roughly how much, and whether you need climate control. Answers stay in this browser. Nothing is saved or sent.

The questions need JavaScript. The situation list and the size notes below cover the same 5x5–10x20 range without it.

Start from your situation

A rule of thumb, not a quote. If two sizes are close, the smaller one costs less every month — including after a rent increase — but only if you can still reach your things.

  • Closet overflow 5x5

    Boxes, bags, and a small piece of furniture. Not a mattress and a sofa together.

  • Studio 5x10

    Bed, a few pieces, and boxes. Step up to a 10x10 if you want an aisle.

  • 1-bedroom 10x10

    The most rented size. One bedroom plus living-room furniture and boxes.

  • 2-bedroom 10x15

    Two beds, a living room, a dining set, and boxes. Appliances may push you to a 10x20.

  • 3-bedroom 10x20

    Most of a household of furniture. About a one-car garage. The kitchen usually stays put.

  • Car or seasonal 10x20

    A car needs a 10x20. A motorcycle, or a season of patio and holiday gear, is usually a 5x10.

What fits, size by size

Square feet are the floor. Ceiling height is often about 8 feet, which is why mattresses go on end. Packing tight saves rent and makes the later increase smaller — until you cannot get the box you need.

5x5

25 sq ft · a large walk-in closet

The smallest unit we price. Fixed fees (admin, protection) are a large share of the true cost here, so a very cheap first month still is not a very cheap six months.

  • Several medium boxes, suitcases, a lamp, seasonal decor
  • A small dresser or a disassembled desk
  • You stand in the doorway to reach things

5x10

50 sq ft · a walk-in closet

The usual studio size, and the right size for a motorcycle or seasonal outdoor gear. A full one-bedroom rarely fits without a fight.

  • Mattress and box spring stood on end, plus a dresser
  • A bookshelf, a bike, and a stack of boxes
  • Or a motorcycle and its gear — not a car

10x10

100 sq ft · a small bedroom

The size city pages lead with. It holds a one-bedroom apartment if you break the table down and keep an idea of where the aisle is.

  • Bed, dresser, sofa, small dining table, boxes
  • A studio, if you want room to walk in
  • The leftover half of a two-bedroom, if one room stays furnished

10x15

150 sq ft · a large bedroom

Two bedrooms of furniture. The monthly rent — and any later increase — is meaningfully higher than a 10x10, so do not take it for the aisle alone unless you will use it.

  • Two beds, a living room, and a dining set
  • Boxes, a desk, and smaller appliances
  • A packed one-bedroom (office, extra sofa, appliances)

10x20

200 sq ft · a one-car garage

The largest size we price. Long enough for a sedan. A three-bedroom household fits; a three-bedroom plus a car usually does not.

  • Furniture from a three-bedroom home
  • A car, with a modest stack of boxes beside it
  • A packed two-bedroom that needs an aisle

Climate control is a price, not a size

Climate control does not give you more floor. It changes the monthly rate. City pages show non-climate street rates unless a note on the row says otherwise. People usually pay the extra for wood furniture, electronics, photographs, instruments, paperwork, and anything fabric that hates heat and humidity.

When you have a real quote, put that monthly number in the city calculator. The increase a few months later applies to the climate rate, not to the teaser.

How size changes the true cost

Operators advertise the first month. The bill that matters is the street rate, plus an admin fee, plus a protection plan, plus whatever the existing-customer increase is once your things are inside. A larger unit raises every one of those rent lines. A smaller unit does not escape the fees — on a 5x5 they can be most of the true average.

Compare the six-month average for the size you will actually rent. A promo on a larger unit can still cost more by month four than a smaller unit at the street rate. How the six-month model works.
